# বুঝে পড়ি কুরআন
## Bengali-first Quranic Arabic: course design and pilot blueprint

Prepared for Sadiq M Alam • 19 September 2026

## 1. Design decision and honest learning promise

Audience: Bengali-speaking adults who already read fully vowelled Arabic fairly well. Delivery: recorded, phone-friendly and self-paced. Lesson length: normally 20–25 minutes, never more than 30. Independent practice: 30 minutes daily, additional to lessons.

The user’s ultimate goal is independent comprehension of approximately 95% of the meaning in previously unseen passages drawn from across the Quran. Preserve this ambition, but distinguish a measured average on a representative assessment from a guarantee about every possible passage. No finite sample establishes 95% understanding of every passage, and some passages permit more than one defensible interpretation.

Proposed starting curriculum: 120 lessons over 24 weeks, with five lessons per week and daily practice. At 25 minutes per lesson, this is 50 hours of lessons plus 84 hours of practice: approximately 134 hours. This is a curriculum budget and pilot hypothesis, not a validated time-to-95% estimate. Learners then continue adaptive reading and targeted lessons until their assessed performance meets the graduation standard. Some will need substantially longer.

The shortest route will be established by pilot evidence: remove activities that do not improve delayed transfer, allow demonstrated mastery to bypass instruction, and add instruction only for diagnosed gaps. Do not advertise a universal minimum before testing.

Suggested public course description:

“ছোট ছোট পাঠ, নিয়মিত অনুশীলন এবং বাস্তব আয়াতের সাহায্যে কুরআনের আরবি বুঝে পড়ার ধাপে ধাপে কোর্স। প্রথমে প্রয়োজনীয় শব্দ ও বাক্যের গঠন; এরপর নতুন আয়াত নিজে বোঝার অনুশীলন।”

Do not advertise “২৪ সপ্তাহে যে কোনো আয়াতের ৯৫% বুঝবেন” without evidence—and a universal any-passage guarantee would remain too strong even after a successful pilot.

## 2. What learners must be able to do

On a new passage, with adequate Arabic context but without translation, glossary, AI or dictionary, learners should be able to:

1. Divide text into meaningful chunks without altering the Quranic text.
2. Recognize familiar vocabulary in its actual inflected and attached forms.
3. Identify who speaks, who acts, who is addressed, and who receives an action.
4. Resolve pronouns using the available context, or identify genuine ambiguity.
5. Identify negation, commands, prohibition, conditions, exceptions, emphasis and temporal relationships.
6. Explain each clause in clear Bengali, then connect the clauses into a coherent meaning.
7. Distinguish what the Arabic explicitly says from interpretation or information supplied by background knowledge.
8. State uncertainty instead of inventing meaning for an unfamiliar form.

Independent linguistic comprehension is the objective. Qualification to derive legal rulings or settle disputed interpretation is outside this course’s assessment.

## 3. The teaching method: দেখুন → ভাঙুন → জুড়ুন → বলুন → মিলিয়ে নিন

This is a proposed teaching synthesis, not a claim of a scientifically validated new method.

| Step | Learner action | Course support |
|---|---|---|
| দেখুন | Read the unaltered Arabic and notice familiar elements | Optional accurate audio; no translation initially |
| ভাঙুন | Identify meaning-bearing chunks and attached pieces | Tap-to-reveal labels and a separate analysis line |
| জুড়ুন | Connect actions, people, descriptions and logical links | Short Bengali questions and relationship arrows |
| বলুন | Explain the meaning in Bengali before seeing the answer | Voice recording or short typed response |
| মিলিয়ে নিন | Compare with a reviewed explanation, diagnose errors, retry | Acceptable paraphrases and specific correction |

Every new concept follows one fully worked example, one partially supported example, then a previously untaught example. Return to it after approximately 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days, adjusted to performance. These intervals are starting settings for the pilot.

### The recurring Bengali questions

- কার কথা বলা হচ্ছে?
- কে কী করছে, অথবা কার সম্পর্কে কী বলা হচ্ছে?
- কাকে, কী, কার, কোথায় বা কখন?
- না, শর্ত, ব্যতিক্রম বা বিশেষ জোর আছে কি?
- আগের কথার সঙ্গে এই অংশের সম্পর্ক কী?
- সব মিলিয়ে নিজের ভাষায় কী বুঝলাম?

Do not force every sentence into an action pattern. Some describe or identify; some quote speech, ask questions or contain an omitted but recoverable relationship.

## 4. Make grammar accessible without removing meaning

| Traditional topic, for the teacher | Learner-facing Bengali explanation |
|---|---|
| Independent and attached pronouns | আমি–তুমি–তিনি; আমার–তোমার–তাঁর; আমাকে–তোমাকে–তাঁকে |
| Nominal clauses | কার সম্পর্কে কী বলা হচ্ছে? |
| Verbal clauses | কে কী করল বা করছে? |
| Idafah | দুই নামের সম্পর্ক: কার/কিসের? |
| Adjectival agreement | কোন শব্দ কাকে বর্ণনা করছে? |
| Case and role markers | শব্দের শেষের সংকেত কখন ভূমিকা বুঝতে সাহায্য করে? |
| Conjugation | কাজের শব্দের শুরু বা শেষ বদলালে কর্তা কীভাবে বদলায়? |
| Derived verb forms | পরিচিত শব্দের গঠন বদলে অর্থ কীভাবে বদলায়? |
| Passive constructions | কাজটি ঘটেছে—কর্তাকে কি বলা হয়েছে? |
| Relative clauses | যে/যারা—তাদের সম্পর্কে বাড়তি কথা |
| Conditional clauses | যদি/যখন—তাহলে কী? |
| Negation, prohibition, exception | নয়, হয়নি, করো না, ছাড়া |
| Ellipsis and word order | কিছু কথা উহ্য আছে কি? আগে আনার কারণে কোথায় জোর? |

Introduce technical labels only as optional reference labels after understanding. Avoid long parsing drills, Arabic composition, conversational Arabic and recitation remediation. Retain receptive recognition of duals, irregular plurals, weak verbs and important endings because the full-Quran goal eventually requires them.

Teach verb time and aspect in context: a form is a clue, not a mechanical English/Bengali tense label. Teach common meanings of particles with counterexamples; never promise one fixed translation per Arabic word. Roots are memory aids, not substitutes for learning actual words and contextual senses.

## 5. Weekly and daily rhythm

Five lesson days per week: 20–25 minutes of lesson plus 30 minutes of practice. Two days: 30 minutes of consolidation or assessment only. A lesson may contain pauses; keep the complete guided experience within 30 minutes. Do not hide a long worksheet inside the stated lesson time.

| Daily independent practice | Minutes |
|---|---:|
| Retrieve previously learned words and forms | 8 |
| Learn a few new meaning units through phrases | 6 |
| Read and unpack a short passage | 10 |
| Explain its meaning in Bengali | 4 |
| Record errors and schedule another attempt | 2 |
| Total | 30 |

On assessment days, the 30 minutes replaces this routine. On heavy review days, introduce no new vocabulary. Offer two 15-minute practice blocks for busy learners. A weekly plan is a pace suggestion, not an attendance requirement.

### Standard 25-minute lesson

| Segment | Minutes | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Retrieval without hints | 3 | Bring back earlier knowledge |
| Read today’s anchor phrase | 3 | Establish the meaning problem |
| Discover one main pattern | 5 | Explain with simple Bengali |
| Two guided examples | 6 | Apply and distinguish |
| New example without support | 5 | Test transfer |
| Exit check and practice instruction | 3 | Identify the next action |

## 6. The 120-lesson curriculum

Each row is one five-lesson module. Lesson 5 in each module includes a short unfamiliar transfer passage and feedback. Vocabulary and retrieval continue throughout; they are not separate stages that delay reading.

| Week / lessons | Five lesson topics, in order | Observable outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 / 1–5 | Entry diagnostic and reading process; meaningful chunks; names/actions/connectors; unpacking Al-Fatihah phrases; first transfer check | Explain a short phrase rather than recall a memorized translation |
| 2 / 6–10 | I/you/he/she/we/they; attached possession; attached object pronouns; person and number contrasts; pronoun transfer | Identify whose and to whom |
| 3 / 11–15 | In/from/to/on; attached bi-/li-/wa-; simple noun relationships; chains of possession or association; phrase transfer | Follow small words and attached pieces |
| 4 / 16–20 | This/that/these; statements without an explicit “is”; descriptions; definiteness and agreement clues; checkpoint A | Distinguish a description from a statement |
| 5 / 21–25 | Frequent completed-action forms; I/we endings; you endings; he/she/they endings; action transfer | Find participants in common completed-action clauses |
| 6 / 26–30 | Ongoing/general action forms; person prefixes and endings; number/gender clues; context and time; checkpoint B | Recognize actions without reciting tables |
| 7 / 31–35 | Requests and commands; plural addressees; familiar supplication patterns; commands versus descriptions; unseen requests | Recognize what someone is being asked to do |
| 8 / 36–40 | la and ma in reviewed examples; lam and lan; prohibition; negation with exception; checkpoint C | Preserve negatives and avoid reversing meaning |
| 9 / 41–45 | Who/what/which; those who; embedded descriptions; question versus relative use; transfer | Connect a main clause with its attached information |
| 10 / 46–50 | And/then; contrast and alternatives; reasons and purpose; consequence and context-sensitive fa; connected reading | Explain how clauses relate |
| 11 / 51–55 | Root families as aids; noun versus action; familiar derived verb contrasts; actor/result words; transfer with distractors | Use word families without guessing meaning from roots alone |
| 12 / 56–60 | Frequent plurals; dual recognition; feminine and plural agreement; irregular plural recognition; midpoint assessment | Read a broader range of people and things |
| 13 / 61–65 | Common weak-verb shapes; shortened forms in context; doubled verbs; visually similar forms; targeted transfer | Recognize frequent forms that depart from simple patterns |
| 14 / 66–70 | Object versus actor; active/passive contrasts; grammatical endings as clues; variable word order; role checkpoint | Avoid swapping who did what to whom |
| 15 / 71–75 | If/when; condition and consequence; time relationships; law and hypothetical contrasts; conditional reading | Preserve conditional meaning |
| 16 / 76–80 | Emphasis; restriction and exception; comparison; common oath patterns and their continuation; checkpoint D | Recognize force and scope of a statement |
| 17 / 81–85 | Quoted speech; speaker changes; pronoun chains; tense shifts in narrative; unseen narrative | Track a story across several verses |
| 18 / 86–90 | Worship and supplication language; guidance and knowledge; moral descriptions; reward and warning; mixed passage | Read recurrent semantic fields in varied forms |
| 19 / 91–95 | Family vocabulary; transactions and obligations; quantities and qualifications; long-clause segmentation; constrained-language reading | Follow qualifications without claiming legal expertise |
| 20 / 96–100 | Polysemy in context; omitted recoverable elements; idiomatic expressions; imagery and linguistic limits; checkpoint E | Know when a literal word list is insufficient |
| 21 / 101–105 | Longer narrative; dialogue reconstruction; discourse links; independent Bengali paraphrase; new narrative assessment | Maintain comprehension across connected text |
| 22 / 106–110 | Longer exhortation; description and contrast; command with conditions; varied passage types; mixed assessment | Transfer beyond familiar short surahs |
| 23 / 111–115 | Vocabulary gap repair; form gap repair; role/pronoun repair; logic gap repair; independent practice | Address each learner’s actual weaknesses |
| 24 / 116–120 | Independent reading process; practice assessment with feedback; separate unseen assessment; correction and extension plan; portfolio and delayed-test preparation | Establish an evidence-based next stage |

Week 23 offers four recorded repair lessons; learners with demonstrated mastery replace the relevant lesson with a reviewed extension passage. Do not skip assessment because a learner knows a surah by heart.

## 7. Passage selection and vocabulary plan

Use Al-Fatihah and familiar short surahs for early motivation, but include unfamiliar short clauses from other surahs from the first module. A short surah is not necessarily linguistically simple. Sequence by actual vocabulary and construction difficulty, not surah length alone.

Create four disjoint passage banks: worked teaching examples; guided practice; unguided formative transfer; withheld graduation assessment. Track prior exposure and memorized translations. Where a withheld passage becomes familiar, replace it with a parallel item.

For passages cut from longer contexts, include sufficient preceding/following Arabic to resolve referents. Mark displayed excerpts as excerpts and retain verse references. Never splice unrelated Arabic fragments and present them as an ayah.

### Vocabulary priorities

1. Meaning-changing connectors, pronouns and attached pieces.
2. Frequent verbs in forms learners will encounter.
3. Frequent nouns and descriptive words.
4. Reusable expressions and collocations.
5. Topic-specific and less frequent words required for broader reading.

Start with a planning budget of roughly 6–8 new units on up to six days per week: at most approximately 864–1,152 introductions over 24 weeks before review-only substitutions. This is a workload ceiling, not a promised retained vocabulary or Quran coverage percentage. A unit may be a word, a new contextual sense, a productive attachment, or a phrase; report these categories separately.

A card contains the Arabic form in context, reference, short Bengali explanation, relevant attachment/form, an audio option and a contrasting example if needed. Test Arabic-to-meaning and form discrimination; do not require Bengali-to-Arabic production as a core goal.

Use the Quranic Arabic Corpus frequency and morphology resources for selection, followed by qualified review. Its lemma listing separates grammatical uses and directs readers to a separate verb concordance; a raw top-word list should not be treated as a complete vocabulary curriculum.

Before making a coverage claim, define tokenization, distinguish lemmas from surface forms and roots, specify whether attached particles count separately, and compute coverage against a fixed Quran text. Report running-word coverage separately from comprehension. This blueprint does not contain a computed 95% coverage list.

## 8. Demonstration lesson: “আমাদের” এবং “আমাদেরকে”

Example for early module 2; simplified instructional Arabic orthography, to be checked against the chosen canonical display before production.

**Anchor:** اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ — Quran 1:6.

Working Bengali rendering: “আমাদের সরল পথে পরিচালিত করুন।” It is an instructional rendering, not a claim to be the only translation.

1. Display the full phrase unchanged. Ask: “এখানে কী চাওয়া হচ্ছে?”
2. On a separate analysis line, show اهْدِ + نَا. Explain: “এই শব্দে নির্দেশনা চাওয়া হচ্ছে; শেষে نَا বলে দিচ্ছে—আমাদেরকে।”
3. Ask: “নির্দেশনা কে চাইছে, কাকে সম্বোধন করা হচ্ছে, আর কার জন্য?” Refer back to the surah’s context rather than treating the phrase as context-free.
4. Contrast رَبَّنَا from 2:201: “আমাদের রব।” Here the attachment expresses a possessive relationship, not “আমাদেরকে।”
5. Reveal آتِنَا from 2:201: “আমাদের দিন।” Have the learner identify “us” after supplying the new action’s meaning.
6. Ask learners to explain why translating every نَا as “আমাদের” or every نَا as “আমাদেরকে” would fail.
7. Use a separate, previously untaught example for the exit check. If the new verb is glossed, label the result as supported pattern transfer, not independent comprehension.

Teacher script:

“আজ বড় কোনো ব্যাকরণের তালিকা মুখস্থ করব না। দেখব, শব্দের সঙ্গে ছোট একটি অংশ যুক্ত হলে কীভাবে ‘কার’ বা ‘কাকে’ বোঝায়। আগে মূল শব্দটি চিনব, তারপর যুক্ত অংশের কাজ দেখব, শেষে পুরো কথার অর্থ বলব।”

Exit assessment: identify the attachment, choose its relationship, paraphrase the full phrase, and explain the choice. Correct reasoning matters more than matching the model Bengali wording.

## 9. Self-paced experience

The first release can use ordinary recorded lessons, downloadable practice sheets, audio and quizzes. An app is optional; the pedagogy must not depend on custom software.

Each lesson package includes a video with Bengali captions, a clean Arabic reading view, one-page recap, vocabulary/phrase cards, guided exercises, a transfer item, reviewed answer explanations and a practice schedule. Text must remain legible on a phone; use a tested Arabic font, correct right-to-left rendering, high contrast and labels that do not rely on color alone.

Provide a translation reveal only after an attempt. Fade supports over time: chunk labels first, then optional word help, then plain Arabic. Distinguish “I recognized it after seeing the answer” from unaided recall.

Adaptive error categories: vocabulary; form; participant role; pronoun reference; logical relationship; discourse/context. Send the learner to a brief explanation plus a fresh example for the specific error. A repeated negative/role error triggers remediation even if total quiz accuracy is high.

Optional AI support may help route learners to approved explanations, replay Bengali instructions or collect practice paraphrases. It must not invent Quran quotations or act as the sole authority for ambiguous interpretations or graduation scoring. The base course works without AI.

## 10. Assessment: defining the 95% aspiration

### A. Keep three measurements separate

- Vocabulary/form recognition: meaning of actual words and forms in context.
- Relationship accuracy: who, to whom, whose, negation, conditions, reference and logical connections.
- Passage comprehension: a Bengali explanation that preserves the passage’s supported meaning.

### B. Proposed graduation assessment

Build an expert-reviewed assessment of at least 30 unseen passages spanning narrative, dialogue, supplication, exhortation, description, and prescriptive language. Sample from across the Quran and across construction and vocabulary difficulty. Use a documented sampling plan; do not choose only easy or short passages. This sample is an initial design, not an established statistically sufficient test.

Administer it in several manageable sessions without translation, glossary, AI or dictionary. Learners may reread the Arabic; this is comprehension, not a speed-reading exam. Supply adequate Arabic context. Use voice or typed Bengali responses so writing skill does not determine the score.

Before testing, reviewers divide each passage into scorable meaning units: participants, actions or descriptions, polarity, qualifications, and clause relationships. Each unit earns 1 for preserved meaning, 0.5 for partially preserved meaning, or 0 for absent/incorrect meaning. Accept semantically equivalent Bengali paraphrases. Identify legitimate interpretive alternatives before scoring.

Provisional graduation rule: at least 95% of available meaning-unit points overall, no passage below 85%, and no unresolved meaning-reversing error. A reversed negation, confused actor/object or lost condition requires correction and a parallel retest. Report per-passage and per-genre results, not only the aggregate. Validate these thresholds in the pilot.

Use two qualified raters for high-stakes responses and adjudicate disagreements. Automated multiple-choice scores alone do not establish 95% comprehension. At four weeks, use a shorter parallel unseen assessment to check retention and report it separately.

Permissible achievement statement: “This learner achieved X% on the specified unaided, unseen Quranic reading assessment.” Do not infer “can understand 95% of every Quranic passage” from it.

### C. Earlier checkpoints

At entry: distinguish decoding ability, memorized translation and new-passage comprehension. At weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24: use progressively richer unseen material and error classification. A provisional 85% formative score with no major meaning reversal allows progression; otherwise provide focused repair and a new equivalent check. This progression rule is separate from the 95% graduation target.

## 11. Beyond lesson 120: retain the full-Quran goal

The extension is part of the course pathway, not an optional upsell required to rescue a misleading promise. Include it in the original scope and communicate that completion depends on demonstrated mastery.

Run four-week reading cycles. Each week includes five short guided passage sessions or micro-lessons plus daily 30-minute practice. Select passages to address observed gaps while preserving breadth. End each cycle with unseen transfer; reassess the mix rather than repeating only favored surahs.

Expand less frequent vocabulary, irregular forms, long-distance references, dense clauses and context-sensitive expressions. Use diminishing support during training and no support during independent assessments. Continue until the learner satisfies the graduation criterion. Do not invent a universal number of extension cycles before seeing real learner data.

Learners already meeting entry criteria for a later module may test out using two different unseen examples plus a later retention check. Reduce repeated instruction, not exposure to necessary language features.

## 12. Content accuracy and editorial process

Use a consistent, verified Quran text—initially Hafs—and label the edition/reading. The first release does not claim to teach all variant readings. The content team should include a Quranic Arabic specialist, a qualified tafsir reviewer for context-dependent meanings, a Bengali language editor and an adult-learning designer; people may hold more than one role if qualified.

Use morphology and syntax references alongside more than one reputable translation and tafsir selected by the review team. Do not treat an English gloss as the final Bengali meaning. Maintain a decision log for ambiguity, alternative accepted meanings and revisions. Keep Quran text, working Bengali gloss, and explanatory commentary visually distinct.

Before recording each lesson, verify Arabic text and reference, analysis, Bengali meaning, distractors, answer key and whether an example actually tests the intended skill. Artificial practice sentences, if used at all, must be explicitly marked as practice, never as Quranic text. Prefer authentic excerpts.

## 13. Pilot before full production

Recruit approximately 20–30 Bengali-speaking adults with varied education and prior exposure. This is an initial usability and learning pilot, not sufficient evidence for universal effectiveness. Record relevant prior knowledge and actual study time.

Produce and test modules 1–4 first: 20 lessons, review cards, transfer items and the first checkpoint. Reserve a disjoint unseen bank before instruction begins. Obtain baseline responses, completion/withdrawal data, error patterns, unaided transfer and delayed retention. Include participants who struggle or withdraw in reporting; do not report only completers’ successes.

Revise confusing explanations and excessive daily workload. Follow with the remaining curriculum in staged production. To establish a claim about speed or 95% outcomes, conduct a longer evaluation with predefined outcomes, representative assessment and a suitable comparison; the small pilot alone cannot establish that claim.

### Production checklist

- 120 lesson objectives and scripts aligned with the map above.
- 120 recorded lessons, Bengali captions and recap pages.
- Verified phrase and vocabulary bank with references and senses.
- Practice bank separated from the graduation bank.
- Formative quizzes, explanation keys and remediation routes.
- Passage-level scoring rubrics and accepted Bengali paraphrases.
- Learner tracker distinguishing vocabulary, structure and comprehension.
- Extension passage cycles and optional focused micro-lessons.
- Editorial version log and correction process.

This document is the course blueprint. Those production assets are specified here, not claimed to have been recorded, built or independently validated.

## 14. Localization after Bengali

Preserve canonical Arabic, lesson objectives, passage identifiers, skill tags and scoring concepts. Localize explanations, paraphrase rubrics, comparisons, captions and learner support with native-language educators and Quranic Arabic reviewers. Do not translate Bengali teaching scripts mechanically: languages differ in word order, pronoun systems and how omitted copulas are understood.

Test a small module in each new language before scaling. “Available worldwide” is a delivery property; “effective for anyone” is not a supportable guarantee. Provide accessible alternatives for audio, visual and literacy needs.

## 15. Sources and evidence boundaries

- [Quranic Arabic Corpus](https://corpus.quran.com/): word-level morphology, syntax and linguistic reference. It is a useful checking resource, not an infallible authority or a ready-made pedagogy.
- [Lemma frequency listing](https://corpus.quran.com/lemmas.jsp): a basis for prioritization; categories and verb treatment must be understood before calculating coverage.
- [Al-Fatihah word analysis](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=1&verse=1): analysis supporting the introductory example.
- [Quran 2:201 word analysis](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=2&verse=201): reference for the attached-pronoun contrast in the example lesson.

The 120-lesson sequence, timing, vocabulary workload, adaptive rules, assessment thresholds and pilot size are proposed design choices. They are not published empirical findings or validated guarantees. No corpus-wide vocabulary coverage calculation or learner trial has been performed for this blueprint.
